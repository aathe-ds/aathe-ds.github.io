Overview

The AA@THE dataset was collected as part of a longitudinal study focused on monitoring older adults in daily life through multimodal wearable and mobile sensing. The study combines continuous behavioral and physiological monitoring with repeated clinical assessments and a structured Adapted Physical Activity (APA) program.

The dataset is designed to support research on longitudinal health monitoring, multimodal behavioral modeling, sleep and activity analysis, and machine learning approaches for health-related prediction tasks in aging populations.

Participants 66 older adults, mean age 72.6 ± 4.4 years.
Duration Approximately 23 months, from April 2024 to March 2026.
Setting Real-world, in-the-wild monitoring with home and free-living data collection.
Modalities Smartwatch, sleep tracker, smart scale, mobile application, EMAs, and clinical tests.

Study design

The study was organized into three phases: a Pilot phase, Phase 1, and Phase 2. Each phase involved continuous remote monitoring and, during structured intervention periods, participation in an APA program delivered by certified trainers. The APA sessions were designed to promote mobility, balance, strength, and cardiovascular health, while also supporting participant engagement with the sensing ecosystem.

Participants underwent repeated clinical assessments at baseline, mid-term, and follow-up visits whenever applicable. These assessments provide clinically grounded anchors for interpreting the behavioral, physiological, and sleep-related trajectories collected through remote sensing.

Phase Approximate period Description
Pilot Apr 2024 – Sep 2024 Initial feasibility and deployment validation phase.
Phase 1 Oct 2024 – Mar 2025 Long-term monitoring period under stable deployment conditions.
Phase 2 Apr 2025 – Mar 2026 Extended monitoring period with additional participants and APA sessions.

Sensing ecosystem

The dataset integrates data from commercial off-the-shelf devices and a custom mobile application. This design supports unobtrusive monitoring in realistic conditions while preserving scalability and participant acceptability.

Source Device / component Main data types
Smartwatch Garmin Venu Sq 2 Steps, activity intensity, heart rate, SpO₂, stress, breathing rate.
Sleep tracker Withings Sleep Analyzer Sleep duration, sleep stages, awakenings, snoring, heart rate, breathing rate, AHI.
Smart scale Garmin Index S2 Weight and body composition indicators.
Mobile app Custom Android / iOS application Daily questionnaires, activity recognition, and location-related data.
Clinical assessments In-person clinical tests Functional capacity, cognitive status, nutrition, sleep quality, mood, and physiological evaluations.

Dataset structure

The dataset is organized by participant. Each participant is associated with a pseudonymous identifier and a folder containing modality-specific files. The structure below summarizes the expected layout. 

data/
  subject_XX/
    activities/
      activities.csv
    locations/
      locations.csv
    scale_data/
      scale_data.json
    sleep_data/
      sleep_data.json
    smartwatch_data/
      smartwatch_data_activity_summary.json
      smartwatch_data_heart_rate.json
      smartwatch_data_respiration.json
      smartwatch_data_spo2.json
      smartwatch_data_steps.json
      smartwatch_data_stress.json
    survey_data/
      survey_data.json

Main folders

Folder Description
activities/ Smartphone-based activity recognition data.
locations/ Anonymized location traces derived from raw GPS data. Coordinates are transformed and spatially perturbed to prevent re-identification while preserving mobility patterns.
scale_data/ Smart scale measurements, including body weight and body composition estimates.
sleep_data/ Sleep summaries and sleep-related physiological indicators collected through the sleep tracker.
smartwatch_data/ Smartwatch-derived activity, physiological, and behavioral data streams.
survey_data/ Daily self-reported questionnaire data collected through the mobile application.

Access policy

Due to the sensitive nature of the data and GDPR regulations, access to the full dataset is restricted to research purposes and requires a formal access procedure. Researchers interested in using the dataset should contact the authors with a short description of the intended research project.

Access will be granted only after the required legal steps have been completed, including the signature of a Data Transfer Agreement between the receiving institution and the authors’ institution.

Restricted access. The full dataset is not publicly downloadable. The public sample only illustrates the dataset structure and format.

Intended use

The dataset can support research on longitudinal multimodal sensing, behavioral modeling in older adults, wearable-based health inference, sleep and activity analysis, and machine learning methods for health-related prediction tasks. It can also be used to investigate the role of historical features and long-term behavioral patterns in predictive modeling.
